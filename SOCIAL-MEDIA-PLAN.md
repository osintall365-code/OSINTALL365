# OSINTALL365 Social Media Launch Plan

## Objective
Build awareness and generate qualified consultation requests without paid advertising.

## Public positioning
OSINTALL365 provides lawful, authorized security analysis, OSINT, digital-exposure review, employee-safety analysis, physical-security observations, internal-investigation support, and monitoring support.

Do not publish personal owner information.
Do not imply law-enforcement affiliation or government authority.
Do not publish customer information, case details, credentials, private data, or unverified claims.

## Content pillars

1. **Security Awareness** — practical observations businesses can use immediately.
2. **Digital Exposure** — public information that can unintentionally create risk.
3. **Employee Safety** — workplace and personal-safety awareness.
4. **Physical Security** — access, doors, badges, visible information, and facility observations.
5. **OSINT Education** — explain lawful public-source research without teaching unauthorized access.
6. **Business Risk** — simple ways organizations can identify and reduce exposure.
7. **OSINTALL365 Services** — occasional direct explanations of what a consultation can cover.

## Initial 10-post queue

### Post 1 — Digital exposure
A business can protect its network and still expose sensitive information publicly.

Public photos, job postings, social profiles, documents, maps, and other ordinary sources can reveal more than intended.

A lawful digital-exposure review can help identify what is visible before someone else notices it.

### Post 2 — Employee safety
Employee safety is not limited to what happens inside the workplace.

Publicly available information can sometimes reveal routines, locations, organizational relationships, or other details that increase risk.

The first step is awareness: know what information is publicly visible.

### Post 3 — Physical security
A security review does not always begin with sophisticated technology.

Doors, access points, badges, posted information, visitor procedures, and everyday habits can create avoidable exposure.

Small observations can identify meaningful improvements.

### Post 4 — OSINT explained
OSINT means researching information that is lawfully available from public sources.

The goal is not unauthorized access. The goal is understanding what an organization or individual has already made publicly accessible and what risks that information may create.

### Post 5 — Badge awareness
A photograph of an employee wearing an identification badge may reveal more than the employee intended.

Before posting workplace photos, consider what is visible, readable, searchable, or reusable.

### Post 6 — Business risk question
Ask your organization one question this week:

**What could a stranger learn about our operations without ever entering our building or accessing our systems?**

The answer may reveal your next security-improvement opportunity.

### Post 7 — Authorized investigations
Good investigative work starts with authorization, defined scope, documentation, and clear limits.

OSINTALL365 uses those principles as a core part of its operating model.

### Post 8 — Digital footprint
Your organization has a digital footprint whether you actively manage it or not.

Old pages, public profiles, employee posts, documents, listings, and other public sources can continue creating exposure.

Periodic review can help reduce unnecessary risk.

### Post 9 — Security culture
Security is not only a technology problem.

It is also a people, process, physical-environment, and information-management problem.

Strong security starts with seeing the whole picture.

### Post 10 — Consultation CTA
Need help understanding what information, physical-security weaknesses, or digital exposure may be visible to the public?

OSINTALL365 provides lawful, authorized security and OSINT analysis.

Every work-order request is reviewed by the owner before any customer work begins.

## Publishing rule

Content may be drafted and prepared automatically. Nothing may claim a customer, result, case, partnership, certification, or completed engagement unless it is verified and approved for publication.

Social publishing requires a connected social-media account/provider. Until one is connected, the content remains a prepared publishing queue.
